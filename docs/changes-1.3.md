# Darklands+ 1.3 in detail

Everything 1.3 changes, by subject. The short list is in `docs/release_notes.md`; `CHANGELOG.md` has the same entries in the order they were made. Each change can be switched off in F12 > Settings (or comes as an option there); "Play the original game" turns them all off.

## The main things

- **Battles that run themselves better** (Improved combat): shooters open fire by themselves, step aside for a clear line, draw their melee weapon when a foe comes beside them or a friend a few paces off is fighting one, and members without orders join a fight beside them. One click gives an order, the orders are buttons at the foot of the field, and the to-hit rules are now shown the way the game uses them.
- **Cards say who tries an option and what it rests on:** the head of the member who tries it, the skills and attributes as heraldic shields, the town's opinion and the party's fame; where any member may speak for the party, a click on another member's chance has that member speak.
- **The world map:** towns on wax seals, windows to find alchemical ingredients and any ware, quests at one place on an arc over it, and a button back to the party.
- **Sound:** the whole soundtrack to play in F12 > Settings > Sound, previews of each device's music and of a real battle's sounds, evener music timing, and previews that leave the device's instruments as they were.
- **Ironman:** one save that the game keeps itself.
- **Restored content:** city politics, cities at war, an ambush of a private war ("Fehde!") and a town's hospice, written by MicroProse and never switched on.
- **More of the original's bugs fixed**, many from the list Arkana Mechanika (Darklands Restoration) keep.

## Battles

- **Improved combat:** a member with no orders who does not shoot joins a fight beside it (a foe on itself, or on a friend a few paces off), unless told to hold. A shooter a few paces from a foe that is fighting one of the party puts the bow away and joins in, rather than stand by and shoot into the melee. Fixed: in a later battle of the same session, shooters opened fire ("shoots" on their box) and then never loosed: they waited for the clock of the last order given in an earlier battle. Fixed: a member sent to fight hand to hand in one battle no longer opened fire by itself in the battles after it; what a battle remembers of its members is now cleared when the next one begins. A shooter whose last arrow is loosed draws its melee weapon and goes at the nearest foe (it stood with the bow). Two shooters stepping aside for a clear line no longer pick spots across each other's way (they met halfway and stood), and a walk aside that goes nowhere is planned again.
- **Battle orders in one click** (F12 > Settings > Battles, each a switch): a click on a member's box in the party panel selects them (it opened their sheet; a right-click does that now, as do F1 to F5). With a member selected, the click on an enemy or on the ground gives the order at once; the original only marked the target and waited for Enter, Space or the next member. Orders that need no target (Halt, a door, the stairs) are given as soon as they are chosen, and an attack chosen for a member already fighting (Vulnerable, Berserk, Parry, Std Attack) is given against that enemy. If selecting the member paused the battle, it resumes with the order; while it is paused, the next member without an order is selected, so the first orders of a battle take one click each, then Space. A label by the pointer says what a click there does ("Click: attack", "Click: walk here", "No clear shot"). A member with a bow, crossbow, gun or thrown weapon has a bow or a sword on their picture in the party panel: click it to choose whether a click on an enemy makes them shoot or fight hand to hand (each character keeps the choice; at first, the better of their two skills decides). A shooter draws the melee weapon by themselves when an enemy comes within reach, when nothing is left to shoot, or when the melee their target is in blocks the line of fire: in the original they went on shooting at point blank, or stood waiting for a clear line, until given a new order. The order buttons no longer repeat what is at hand otherwise (Enemy Info, which pointing at an enemy shows, and "<name> finished", which the click now does). Each order button has a picture beside its name (a sword for Std Attack, a shield for Parry, footprints for Walk towards, a white flag for Surrender ...). In a window with no room for a right margin (narrower than 16:9), the order buttons appear in a strip at the bottom of the battlefield, so the right-click menu is never needed.
- **Battle controls:** the selected member's orders are buttons at the foot of the field, grouped by what they do (Attack, Move, Here, Potions and items, Battle), and only as wide as they need; "Std Attack" is "Standard". Fast forward and Resume stand at the top of the right margin, always in the same place (Resume dimmed while the battle runs), so selecting a member no longer pushes the margin's bars down.
- **Attack and defense, told right:** Darklands+ had the game's two to-hit rules the wrong way round. Hand to hand, the chance to hit is 50 + 2/3 of the attacker's melee rating minus (the defender's melee rating + the shield's value against blows; a flail's blow passes any shield); a shot or a throw goes against the shield's value against shots plus twice the target's Agility (less under a load). The pointer's battle info now shows "Melee rating" (the one number that counts both ways hand to hand) and "aim" (for a missile weapon), where it said "Attack" and "defence" of the wrong numbers; the shields' values against blows and against shots are no longer swapped, and the weapons' tooltips say what skill or Strength short costs (melee rating, or aim).
- **Battles, walking in a crowd** (with "Fighters find their way around obstacles" on, F12 > Settings > Battles, now one switch for the party and the enemies alike; "Better pathfinding for enemies" is gone): stress-tested with traces of whole battles (eight pirates on four, wolves in a clearing, bandits all round, the party walking into the melee).
  - A fighter whose step was blocked by somebody stood until its next plan came round, up to two and a half seconds, though the one in its way had stopped to fight; it now plans around whoever blocked it and asks again within half a second.
  - On a new route the walker first took the step it had lined up from the old one, then went on from the new one's second step: a step off the route, often into somebody. The new route now starts from its first step.
  - A walker going for a target that has moved on now heads for where the target is, not first to where it was.
  - An enemy no longer takes a long way round a crowd (walks of 150 to 200 steps for a target that would be elsewhere by then): it waits, or picks another target, as the game does when it finds no way.
  - Planning a route no longer makes the battle stutter: the floor's part of each collision test is asked of the game once per spot and kept, the fighters' part is done natively (the same test, checked against the game's in whole battles), a target with no free side is known at once instead of after combing the map, and a step that touches the target counts as reaching it whoever else it touches (as for the game's walker). The longest planning in a battle went from 40 ms or more to a few ms.
  - When a member's enemy fell, the game measured the walking distance to every other enemy with its route trackers (up to 40 ms in one tick, a hitch at each kill); it now uses the same quick search (at most 250 steps, as the trackers).
  - The shooters' "step aside for a clear line" (experimental, off by default) never noticed a line opening while walking towards the target: it read the wrong argument of the step.
- **Enemy tooltips:** each of a member's weapons against the enemy's armor in its own color: green pierces, amber does half damage, red is mostly deflected.
- **Battle tooltips beside the pointer** (what a click would do, an enemy's info) start past the game's hand, not over it.
- **Battles:** a member's sheet opened in a battle keeps the battle's margins (the log and the enemies) instead of the town's. Closing it no longer starts the field scrolling because the pointer rests on the top edge where the Close button was. The margin's list of enemies includes those the log has named even when they are out of view (it said "All down." while enemies beyond the view were still fighting).

## Cards, towns and saints

- **Cards: who tries it, and what it rests on:** beside an option's chance, the head of the member who tries it (every member's when each tries), and the skills and attributes it rests on as heraldic shields, the town's opinion of the party (a medal) and its fame (Fame's trumpet). Pointing at them says it in words. Where the chance is whoever speaks for the party, an arrow at its end slides out every member's chance: a click on one has that member speak (also with "The best member speaks" off, where the leader speaks otherwise).
- **Alchemists' formulae:** on the alchemist's two formula options (trading, and the free trade an alchemist sometimes offers when asked to sell), each member is offered with their own chance: click the one who should speak. The game offers a formula the speaker does not know and teaches it to the speaker, so letting the best talker speak gave the formula to the wrong member, or found nothing worth trading. (Buying from the list was never affected: it names who learns each formula.)
- **Calling on a saint:** when there is no chance, the screen says why: the member's Virtue is below what the saint asks (more favor will not help), or the saint asks more favor than is spent or than the member has. It used to say only "spend more favor".
- **Go to (G):** the night and side-street wordings of the town's ways are all known ("a lane where the signs have alchemical symbols" and others): no more "not found in this city" for a place the card offers.
- **Go to:** a town hall's or a lord's chance of an audience is the same number the card shows (it said one more). The market stalls' chance of a quest (merchants, foreign traders, pharmacists) says "after shopping": their offer comes as the party leaves the stalls, one time in seven, and not again for three days when none comes.
- **The inn's day:** a member making potions says which and how many ("Makes 3 al-Razi's Noxious Aroma"), not the game's "Make 3 potions"; a teacher's row gives the member's level in the skill now ("now 10, to 74"); an inn's wages are three small coins touching, drawn like the money's.
- **A room at the inn, camping:** spending a day no longer looks like the game froze. The game's clock ran for four seconds under the page, unseen; now the page shows the hours going by itself (the bell, the day, a bar), in about a second, and a click or a key skips it. A week, or Until rested, shows each day the same way, faster ("Day 3 of 7"), and a click skips to the end.
- **Inn and camp:** "Earn a little money" shows a small heap of coins, one over another.
- **The town icons** (margin, world map) show what most towns lack: a university (13 of 92) or a cathedral (48). An alchemist (80), a physician (88), docks (79) and the banks' offices (all 92) are in nearly every town; the world map still finds each.
- **Right margin:** Fame, the same everywhere, stands above the town's name; the town's Reputation beside its name. Pointing at Reputation says what the game does with it: many of the town's cards weigh it, and below 0 the shops ask more and pay less (up to 9% from about -20 down); the earlier wording about a town that "hates" the party below -30 was not the game's and is gone.
- **Reputation and fame in the right margin** carry their pictures (the medal, Fame's trumpet), the same as under a card's chance that rests on them.
- **New encounters:** one met on the way out of a town, while the party still stands by its gate, no longer offers Go to in the margin.
- **Quests:** a place without a name of its own reads "the pagan altar north-west of Hall".

## The world map

- **World map, top right:** "Find alchemical ingredients" and "Find any ware" (each a window of its own), then "Find a place", then "Towns with ..." and the Map Legend (the kinds of place; the quest, party and unvisited-town entries are gone). "Snap to party" at the lower right centers the map on the party.
- **Find any ware:** what a shop is expected to sell follows the way most visits go: by day the arms-making street sends the party straight to a swordsmith selling swords and the like and an armorer selling armor (the guild's own shopfronts, the other way in, sell every weapon and armor). A list seen in a shop replaces the guess for that shop. Fixed: the finder said Nürnberg's swordsmith sold body chainmail.
- **World map, quests at one place:** several quests leading to the same place stand on an arc of fixed size over it (more quests: a more crowded arc, never a long row across the map); the one under the pointer is drawn over the others. Also on the travel map.
- **World map:** towns on a wax seal, sized by the town (small, medium, large), washed out until visited and then pressed with a ring; castles share the villages' and ruins' color.
- **World map:** the view stays on the map instead of scrolling into black past its edges (the west edge stops beside the panel, so the places under it can be seen). A place picked in Find, or with a click, shows in the panel what the pointer shows (what it has, its notable shops, and its saints, formulae and teachers once known), with the journey and Travel there first; a long panel scrolls. Times on the way read as estimates ("22 hours", "1.4 days"), and Find no longer cuts what a place is in the middle of a word.

## Items and the party

- **Shops:** pointing at a weapon, armor or shield says what the member's load would come to with it worn, and which load that is (light, normal, heavy, overloaded) with what it does; the line turns orange for a heavy load and red when overloaded. A long description no longer runs over the hint line at the bottom.
- **Character sheet with the shared pack:** a member's own potions are listed with the member, above the pack (they were shown under "The party's pack"); the pack's heading is a band across the list, centered, with a pack drawn beside it.
- **Ammunition that runs out (Endless ammunition or Endless thrown weapons off), with the shared pack:** each member's arrows, quarrels, balls and thrown weapons are their own, listed with them like potions, not in the party's pack; ones picked up from the loot or taken from a store room go to whoever shoots them, and dragging them onto another member hands them over.
- **Musical instruments** show no quality: the only thing that asks for one, St. Cecilia, asks only whether one is carried.
- **Ingredients are all signs now,** in one thick line, as in the alchemists' tables: the planets' signs for the metals and their ores (all in one pewter, gold in gold), each substance's own sign (orpiment, alum, gum, putrefaction for Marsh Vapor, Water's triangle with an R for Aqua Regia), and for the herbs, which never had one, signs made the same way (two seeds joined, the berry crossed out, the mandrake's leaves, head and forked root, the camomile's heart and petals). Nikel is copper ore turned upside down (Kupfernickel: the ore that looks like copper's and yields none).
- **An ingredient's description** shows the potions it goes into as pictures (on the character sheet too), no longer as a list in words; the alchemy screen's ingredient pictures are no longer clipped.

## Sound and music

- **The soundtrack** in F12 > Settings > Sound: every piece of the game's music, played through the device chosen, with play, pause, the time played, its length and a bar to move through it. Every piece's length is listed from the start (measured on each device beforehand: no silent run ahead the first time a piece is played), and with "music in menus" off a preview or a piece no longer stays silent.
- **Hearing a sound device's battle sounds** (F12 > Settings > Sound, Preview: Battle Sounds) plays ten seconds of a real battle as it was heard, each sound at its time: the misses and parries, knock-backs, a bow, a death (no longer a few sounds one after another).
- **Sound previews fixed:** a sound driver changed for a preview (F12 > Settings > Sound) now sets its device up: its setup went nowhere before, so the MT-32 kept the last driver's instruments (the music afterwards played on wrong ones, as if half were missing) and the FM chip's instruments were not loaded (odd sounds in a preview). After a preview, the piece that was playing starts again only if it had not already ended (music no longer starts out of nowhere in the menu).
- **MT-32 and CM-32L:** no choice between them any more (they sound alike): an MT-32 pair is used when both are found. MIDI out to a real synthesizer or Munt is a device of its own, under OK.
- **Music while the Darklands+ window is open** (the previews, the soundtrack, music in menus): the music driver's timer ticks go to it a millisecond at a time instead of in bursts after each frame, and every note, MT-32 or FM, is timed by when its tick fell due (in the game too): the previews played unevenly and could slow down.
- **FM music** (Sound Blaster, AdLib): each change to the chip at its moment, instead of every change in 23 ms of sound at once.
- **MT-32:** the settings no longer list every folder searched for the ROM files.

## Pictures, tooltips and the interface

- **Skills and attributes are heraldic shields wherever they are shown** (the inn's teachers, the cards, the character sheet, making a character ...), not only on the character sheet.
- **Pictures:** Agility is a line of steps (treads and risers), Charisma a rose, Stealth a mask over the eyes, Streetwise a sitting cat, Speak Common a speech bubble, Speak Latin an Ionic column, Read & Write a quill on a sheet of paper, Polearms a halberd and Thrown Weapons an axe in flight; a quest's reward is a purse, an inn's wages small coins.
- **Tooltips** that belong to something on screen (a card option's chance, a member's chance, a shop's item) open beside it, below or above, never over it nor over the pointer.
- **One pointer over a Darklands+ window:** with the first-time tips or another window of Darklands+ over the game, only the system's pointer shows, where a click lands (the game's hand was drawn too, and could sit off the point a click went to).
- **Margins:** their tooltips (a saint, a teacher, a quest) no longer show on top of Go to, the world map or the F12 window when one of those lies over the margin.
- **Cards:** a card's tooltip (the saints or items an option takes) no longer stays over the F12 window, the world map or Go to, and the saints an option takes are listed by their whole names ("St. Christina the Astonishing", not "S.Christina").
- **F12:** the Weapons and Enemies tables are in a tab of their own, Appendices; at the start screen, the Editor and the other pages no longer act as if a game were under way.
- **A key that closes a page of Darklands+** (Enter on the Save page, a key on enemy info) no longer reaches the game afterwards too: an Enter that saved could then choose the card's first option.
- **Controller button prompts:** while a controller is in use, the screens' Leave, Close, Cancel, Save and Load buttons, the margin's hints, the world map, F1 and a card waiting to go on show its buttons, drawn as an Xbox, PlayStation or Nintendo controller has them (as the controller is; F12 > Settings > Controller can set one). "Go to a place here" in the margin opens Go to with a click (a controller has no G), and pressing the right stick fast-forwards a battle (Z).
- **First-time windows:** tips at the first battle with Improved combat (what a click does, and the four attacks with their pros and cons), and a word before the intro about the MT-32 when none is found (naming the ROM files it needs). "Never show again" can be ticked from the fourth start, and says until then how many more times the window will come.
- **Comic Sans**, as an eighth typeface for the game's text (F12 > Settings > Text). We are so sorry. Windows' own Comic Sans where the system has it, else Comic Neue, which comes with Darklands+.

## Saving

- **Ironman** (F12 > Settings > Preset, for the next new game, any preset): the game keeps one save and saves over it itself, on every card, on arriving at a place, before each battle and when you quit or close the window (once it can: not in a battle or a shop). Loading back is off while that game runs (the game's Save, Ctrl+S and Save now save over its one save); the start screen offers it as "Continue (Ironman)"; when the party's journey ends, its save is deleted.
- **Saves backed up:** the first start of each version copies the saved games into "Darklands+ save backups" (in the Darklands folder, or Darklands+'s data folder where that cannot be written).

## Restored content (F12 > Reconstructed Content, experimental, off by default)

- **City politics, restored (experimental, off by default):** the political campaign MicroProse wrote, coded and never switched on. With F12 > Reconstructed Content > "City politics" ticked, now and then a city has a season of unrest: rumors in the news and the agitators' guild lead to the rebels' leader or the government's man, the party takes a side, posts handbills against the other side's thugs, sways the guilds, and a war council sets the day of a march on the town hall (or fortress) or of the arrests; the other side may strike first. It ends with a rally, a march past the guards, a siege or an escort of prisoners through an angry crowd, a reward or a flight, and news of it afterwards. What Darklands+ had to write or put right for it (the season's setup was never called, the war council led to "Sorry, this option isn't working yet.", the party's own side could never set the decisive day, a dozen bugs in code never run) is in docs/cut_politics.md.
- **Cities at war, restored (experimental, off by default):** MicroProse coded cities that prepare for war and stand sieges (five cards, 78 texts, and prices, news, docks and a soldiers' street that change with them) but nothing ever started a war, and the war's arrival card loaded no text. With F12 > Reconstructed Content > "Cities at war" ticked, now and then a city prepares for war for a few weeks, and some then stand a siege: arriving, the party finds a gate that turns travelers away (walk in as if you belonged, talk, a farmer's hay cart, alchemy, a saint, a fight), or tries the walls, where patrols take it for spies (a chase, a sally port, a sewer grating, a bribe). Inside, Soldiers' Road opens in the arms-makers' quarter, with an outfitter selling every kind of weapon. The margin says when a town is preparing for war or at war. What was put right (the arrival card's text, a picture never drawn, option lines that ran the next one's choice) is in docs/cut_content.md.
- **"Fehde!":** an ambush on the road by soldiers of a private war, written by MicroProse for the mercenaries' ambush and never used, now comes up half the times that ambush does (experimental, off by default: F12 > Reconstructed Content).
- **The hospice:** a town's hospice, written by MicroProse with no code to show it, is open by day from the church district (Go to lists it): give alms, work a day beside the nuns (Healing), have the party's wounds tended for a fee, or beg to be tended for nothing (experimental, off by default: F12 > Reconstructed Content).
- **Restored cut content has a page of its own:** F12 > Reconstructed Content. What MicroProse wrote, coded or left unfinished and never switched on, and Darklands+ put back, is there, each a switch, all off unless ticked and in every preset: city politics, cities at war, "Fehde!", the hospice, Black Cloud in battle and the Honor Roll of Glory. Black Cloud and the Honor Roll were on by default before; they go off once on updating (tick them again to keep them).
- **What the game holds unused** (texts with no code, drafts, placeholders, a barber-surgeons' guild, a hospice, the fair's gate, the developers' jokes) is catalogued in docs/cut_content.md.

## Bugs of the original game fixed

- **More of the original's bugs fixed**, from the list Arkana Mechanika (Darklands Restoration) keep of them (each can be switched off in F12 > Settings > Fixes):
  - climbing a city wall at night: everyone climbing without a rope always made it, and the best climber sent up with a rope always fell (both chances read a number that only a failed climb by everyone sets, and that climb could never fail); now the climber's chance is his own and everyone's is the weakest's, and the cards show them;
  - claiming friendship with a Hussite band succeeded one time in a hundred, whatever the chance shown;
  - an audience the council's clerk or the lord's steward "fits you into tomorrow" was never booked: the next day the party had to ask again like anyone; now the audience is certain;
  - four heavy blows landing on one fighter at once (a member mobbed by a pack) healed instead of hurting: the wounds waiting to be dealt wrapped round past 127;
  - a castle level whose only room to start in was the gate room froze the game;
  - riding away from the Wild Hunt or a wolf pack never counted the mounts' quality, as it was meant to;
  - the Templars' writing desk taught formulae the reader already knew; a potion thrown at footpads was never used up; the witches' ointment could not be used to flee the sabbat between waves; the tatzelwurm's death text came even when nobody was eaten; crossing the Cavern of Fire and Hail under armor, and clearing the spider warehouse, healed the party instead of hurting it; a village Schulz's "Please accept this florin" gave none; the inn's stablemaster asked about selling the party's mounts only when it had none; the spider warehouse's owner had no name ("I am . Are ye hardy adventurers?"); paid for a robber knight, the Hanse's guide or the village's Schulz who came along was said to leave by the toss of a coin (or when he had never come), then stayed on: now he goes home, and only if he came;
  - a failed search for formulae at a university shut the saints' question for a week instead of its own;
  - an item a card gives the whole party (out of a dungeon, from footpads) came twice to each member who already had one;
  - a member on guard at camp skilled with impact weapons (clubs, maces and hammers) did not make the camp safer, as one skilled with any other weapon does;
  - setting up a battle, an empty band of enemies made the game drop every band after it too;
  - a town put on alert by a fight with its gate guards (or by a rising) never remembered it: a standing of -10 or worse is now enough to find the guards, the docks and the town hall hostile while the alert lasts, as the game meant; and a second fight at the gate costs standing again;
  - St. Cosmas and St. Damian, who teach each other, taught nobody (and slipped a formula to the fifth member instead);
  - buying with no room in anyone's pack took the money for nothing, and taking from a store room into a full pack lost the item: both are refused now;
  - the friars' day jobs (preaching, mendicant) were never offered; three cards that weigh two attributes together (in a dungeon, in town, in the country) took the first one twice; a lowering given to the whole party stopped at the first member boosted above it; the docks told a party short of the fare "You need a missile weapon"; a rich party could find a church's tenth greyed (only part of the purse was counted); trouble on the way to a town's grove by night led to the night's grove after the day had come; shots in a battle with clouds on the field (Black Cloud, smoke) were tested against a cloud too many, and after a cloud the shot missed, against a line made of leftover values;
  - after a battle, more than 255 of one kind of loot (arrows, bolts) wrapped round and the rest was lost; a hireling killed in battle left his contract behind, whose end could later send away whoever had taken his place; Esc on a childhood's points while making a character went back to a list that no longer applied (it now leads on to the professions); sneaking into a guild's quarters (city politics) made the whole party practice once per member; castle passages had their torches placed past their ends;
  - four more saints do what their texts say (the sainttexts switch): St. Edward the Confessor heals Endurance rather than raising it past its maximum, and his price is a true quarter of the money (a party with more than 273 florins paid less); St. Gerlac gives Charisma and every weapon skill, not only those in hand; St. John Climacus and St. Jude raise every skill, the weapons and Riding too;
  - in the city politics (experimental): the city lord's boons of a formula or a rare saint could not be had (and the saint would have been the wrong one); the faction's house by day told the government's party the rebels' news too; potions that "make success assured" there always failed; knocking at your own side's back door showed a meaningless chance; and the handbill thugs on a main street were fought twice, the first fight's outcome lost.
- **A church's tenth:** giving a tenth in a town church raised the Divine Favor of "member" 40 or so (the best Religion's value taken for a member), past the party into the game's memory; it now goes to the member best at Religion, as meant. Sneaking into a guild leader's house at night no longer names a member by the lowest Agility value (a garbled name). Found by Arkana Mechanika (Darklands Restoration).

## Drawing

- **Lighter frames:** the braided frames round the margins and the picture are drawn with a quarter of the triangles they took (at 4K a frame of the inn went from some 1.35 million indices to 360 000), the same to the eye. Anything the graphics driver refuses while drawing is now written to the log ("renderer: ..."), to track down the washed-out inn and camp seen on some machines.
- **The ornate frames and backdrops are kept as triangles on every computer** (they were kept as pictures drawn on the graphics card, which showed as a cream wash over the inn, the camp and the margins on some machines, a laptop among them); display.draw_cache 2 still asks for pictures.
- **The character sheet draws faster:** the game's text hidden under it is no longer drawn (three quarters of what the sheet drew each frame).

## Other fixes

- **The inn's party screen:** once a character had been left in another town, Darklands+'s screen listed them anyway, out of step with the game's own list, so Add, Leave at the inn, Heraldry or the colors could act on a different character than the one chosen. It now lists exactly who the game lists (the party, and those kept in this town). A member who joined in another town cannot stay behind here (the game's rule): the button is greyed and says to use the inn's "allow ... to retire" instead.

## Withdrawn

- **The New Player Guide is withdrawn** (with the start screen's note, the margin's hint, the welcome's button and the menu entry that led to it): its statements were checked by reading the game's code, and some were still wrong (a new party's money: the 200 florins were a MicroProse test card's, not the game's start). It comes back only when each statement has been seen to hold in the game itself.
