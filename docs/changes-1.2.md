# Darklands+ 1.2 in detail

Everything 1.2 changed, screen by screen. The short list is in `docs/release_notes.md`; `CHANGELOG.md` has the summary since 1.0.

More of the game's screens are drawn by Darklands+ itself (each can be switched off in F12 > Settings > Helpers; "Play the original game" turns them all off). The **shops** show everything for sale and everything the member carries in two lists, with Buy and Sell on each row (Shift: ten / the whole stack; a click on a member's coin makes them the one who pays), prices you cannot pay in red, what is in hand or worn marked red at its left, and a strip at the bottom describing whatever the pointer is on instead of tooltips over the lists. The **character sheet** is drawn anew: the game's paper doll, put together from its own pictures as you equip things, the load under it, and beside it the things (those in use first; the quality only where it can differ) with Ready, Unequip, Drink and Drop (with Undo) on each row; a thing dragged onto a member in the party panel is given to them (Shift: one of a stack). The attributes and skills fit on one page in the original's groups, with their signs. The **saints** of the whole party are on one page: who knows each, a choice of what they help with, and the saint's statue from the game's prayer screen beside its legend and effect. The **formulae** of the whole party likewise: who knows each, the ingredients against what the party carries, and each alchemist's chance. It works in battle too. The game's **menus** (Game, Orders, Attack, Party) open with a right click or F10 and stay open until you choose an entry or click elsewhere, instead of only while the right button is held; their entries are still the game's own. At the bar's right end, **Darklands+** has a menu of its own: its window (F12), the quests, the world map, the helpers (settings), the guide and the keys of the moment (F1).

**Save** asks for the comment on a page that lists the saves there are, and **Load** lists them all to choose from, each with where and when it was made in the game, who is in the party and how long ago it was written (the saving and loading are still the game's own). A click on a save in the Save page's list makes the new save take its place (the old one is deleted once the new one is written; a click on it again keeps it), and the Load page has a Delete for the save chosen (a second click deletes it for good; never the save in play). The **party panel** at the left is drawn anew wherever it shows: labelled Endurance, Strength and Divine Favor bars with their numbers, the order of each member in a battle in a word (attacks, flees, halts, no line when a shooter has no clear shot, no shot when there is nothing left to shoot, stairs, picks a lock ...; pointing at it says more), and a picture: your own portrait (portraits\NAME.png in the Darklands+ folder), or the member's battle figure in their colours (from the start, not only after a battle), or the herald (F12 > Settings > Helpers). The **loot** after a battle shows the whole pile, takes a whole stack in one click, and has Take all and Share out; the thing under the pointer is described below the lists, with about what a shop pays for it.

**Making a character** is one page per step (name and gender, childhood, attributes, profession, skills) with the character as it stands beside it and what the childhood or profession under the pointer would bring: the attributes after it (and what the years past thirty cost), each skill's gain and the room left, the virtues and saints learned; + and - on each attribute or skill, the name typed in place.

**Choosing the party** of a new game is one page too: the four places with each member's figure in their colours and their Endurance, Strength and Divine Favor, the characters kept (click to choose, double-click to add or drop), and the chosen one's attributes, best skills and what they know and carry, with Add or Drop, the sheet, the figure, herald and colours, and Delete (asked twice); New character and Begin along the bottom; each member added gets a look of their own (a woman the woman figure, everyone the next colours) instead of all the same tall man. Under the four places, the party at its best: each skill outside weapons with the party's best value (weak ones in red), and under the pointer who that is and what the skill is for. The **inn's party screen** (find somebody to join) is the same page: those kept at the inn, Add or Stays here, a new character, the looks and the sheet, Done. At an inn's party screen too: a member who retired and joins again comes back with their own figure and colours (the game gave everyone who joined the tall man in the same colours), and a newcomer gets a look of their own.

**Party Info** shows the party's fame and its title, the time, where the party is, its wealth, and its reputation in every city at once (by reputation or by name), instead of one town at a time under the pointer.

**Change Marching Order** is a list to move members up and down, instead of clicking each in turn.

**A room at the inn** (and a camp) is one page: each member with strength and divine favor and the day chosen for them, the activities they can choose (greyed, with the reason, where not), the town's teachers with their fees a day, what a day costs against the purse, and A day, **A week**, **Until rested** (days until nobody is regaining strength or praying, at most 14) and Leave (several days pass without the hourglass for each); the choices are still the game's own.

**Enemy Info** (E in a battle) shows every kind of foe on the field with its picture, how many there are and still fight, its armour, shield and weapons, and whether each of your weapons pierces its armour, is halved or is deflected (the original's two kinds were drawn under the new party panel, hidden, while the battle waited for a key). The travel map's messages (entering a robber knight's land, a blighted country ...) are shown in the cards' style, and the click that closes one no longer walks the party.

**Battles** say more. Z fast-forwards (six times the pace; a button in the margin too). Experimental, off by default (F12 > Settings > Helpers): a shooter can be sent at any foe; blocked by a friend, a foe or a corner, the shooter steps aside to a spot with a clear line, or walks towards the foe until one opens (a potion thrown keeps the game's own rule). The party panel says each member's order in a word (attacks, shoots, flees, halts, "no line", stairs ...). The end says "Victory! The battle is won." (or how else it ended) for three seconds instead of a still field for eight. In a castle, dungeon or mine, the margin lists the foes still waiting there, floor by floor, marks the master of the place (whose fall, once his fight is over, wins it) or names the floor where he waits, and the battle pauses when a group of them comes out to fight (F12 > Settings > Helpers > Battles; each of these can be switched off).

**Calling on a saint** shows the saint's statue, legend and what the saint does, the divine favor to spend with - and +, and the chance the saint hears, with Pray (for a chosen member where the saint helps one) and Don't pray. The **store room** of a town shows everything stored and everything the member carries in two lists, with Take and Put on each row (one or the whole stack) and Put away for everything not in use.

**Alchemy** lists every formula the alchemist knows, with its ingredients needed and at hand (and, for one that is short, where the party has seen it sold: here, or the nearest town and its price; failing that, the nearest town whose shops will have it, marked "expected": a town's wares are fixed for the whole game; both can be switched off), the batches with - and +, and the chance of success with its parts. The stock, prices, equipping and chances are still the game's own.

**Arrows, quarrels and balls never run out** (on by default, F12 > Settings > Helpers): the party's shots take nothing from the pack (one in the quiver is enough) and leave nothing to pick out of the loot afterwards; enemies shoot and lose theirs as before. Javelins, throwing axes, throwing knives and darts never run out either (a switch of their own).

In a town, **Go To** (G) marks the house a task leads to with the task's picture, its words in the tooltip: the giver who waits with a reward, or the office above the marketplace that keeps papers to be got.

Also: in a town from 19:00 to 5:00 (the game's night, which the margin used to call evening until 21:00) the margin says "Night in the streets"; pointing at it tells of the night watch and the cutthroats of the side streets, with the party's chance of slipping past them (its Stealth and Streetwise). Thrown javelins, axes, knives and darts go back to their throwers after a battle (the 1.1.1 version did not recognise them; what was given back, or why not, is written in the log); the loot's rows say Take all and Take 1; travel to a town no longer stops beside it (a site's edge that enters nothing); a member's sheet opened in a battle no longer ends the battle log; item and saint tooltips stay inside the window; F10 opens the menus on the cards and the new screens too, and Alt+S, Alt+L and the game's other Alt keys work there; in a battle the member being given orders has the orders the game allows as buttons in the right margin (Std Attack, Vulnerable, Berserk, Parry, Missile, Walk, Halt ...); a click or the pointer on the margins no longer reaches the game (a battle map scrolled, a click walked the party to the picture's edge); in a battle the right margin no longer repeats the party's bars when the new party panel shows them.

Fixed: with the New encounters mod on, travel could stop the game with "pics\xhouse3.pic: No such file" (the developers' test card, card 0) or meet the town guards' challenge in open country: the mod lost the map's own choice of what comes next when no new encounter was due. A guard also sends any request for card 0 on to the map or the town.

Fixed (a bug of Darklands+ 1.0 to 1.1.1): with the "Blessings" fix on, a saint's lowering of an attribute (several saints lower Perception, Intellect, Agility or Charisma for a day as the price of their help) was recorded as nothing, so it never wore off. It is now given back when it runs out (never past the maximum), and a boost that ends while a lowering still runs takes off only its own share. Points already lost this way can be put back in F12 > Editor (shown when switched on in F12 > Settings > Helpers).

Fixed (a bug of 483.07, the "Blessings" fix): a saint's boost to endurance or strength that a night's rest or a wound had used up was taken off again when it ran out, so resting while blessed cost endurance and a badly wounded member could die when the boost ended; now only what is still above the maximum is taken off.

Fixed (a bug of 483.07): a merchant who sends the party for debt notes names who holds them, the Medici or the Fugger, but the game hid them in another office above the marketplace, so breaking into the office named found nothing; they are now in the office named. The quest log says who holds papers to be got in a town, and that the offices above the marketplace are reached at night.

Fixed (a bug of 483.07, the "A formula's risk the right way round" switch): a formula is marked low, medium or high risk, and the risk decides how a failed batch at an inn, in a village or a hideout goes wrong (half of them do): an explosion (the party hurt, damages to pay, no room at that inn for four months) or a stink (a month). The game had it backwards: a low-risk failure exploded three times in four, a high-risk one half the time; now one time in four, three or two. The alchemy page says so under the pointer on a formula's risk, and the inn page that a failed batch can blow up there and not in a camp in the wild.

Fixed (a bug of 483.07): the inn's Train or Study could list no teachers and show a cost like "-4096pfs" (and a purse that grew), then pass the day with nothing learnt, when another town's teachers were still about; it now lists the town's own teachers and charges their fee.

Fixed (a bug of 483.07): when acid or fire wore down a member's armour in battle, every identical spare that member carried (identical things are kept as one stack) lost quality with it and was destroyed with it; now only the armour being worn suffers. The skills a battle, a lesson or a card raised are no longer hidden behind the party panel (where the game waited for a key unseen): each shows on the member's box for a few seconds and goes into the log. The shops, the loot, the store room and the sheet say what an ingredient goes into (and which of those formulae the party knows), what rope, lockpicks and instruments are for, and that the game has no use for torches, candles, lanterns, spikes, grapnels and clocks. At an alchemist who sells formulae, the option lists the formulae themselves with their prices and who in the party knows each already; its list names each formula (not just the potion), marks the members who know it, and its tooltip gives that formula's difficulty, its place among the potion's three formulae, who knows it and its ingredients. The formula tooltips everywhere say who in the party knows the formula. In a shop that sells ingredients, **Formulae** lists the formulae the party knows that use them: how many batches the party can make with what it carries, what a batch takes and what it costs here, and +1 / +5 buy what one or five batches more need (and say what must be found elsewhere). In a shop and in the loot, a weapon or an armour is set against what the chosen member has in that place now: the armour worn there (harder or softer, better or less well made), the shield carried, or the weapon in hand with the member's skill for this one and whether it is too heavy. Should Darklands ever stop on an error, Darklands+ now says so (instead of "the party's journey has ended") and no longer enters the party in the Honor Roll. After a fight in a street or a dungeon, the margin says how to go on (walk out of the place). A card's tenth option is chosen with 0.

**The chronicle** (F12 > Journal > Chronicle) has a list of the days on its left, newest first and grouped by month (the newest month open), each day opening to the places of that day (a day spent in one place names it beside the date); a click shows that day, its texts in the order they were read under the name of each place, each with the hour it was read (kept from this version on: older entries have none), and Day before / Day after leaf through the days. The search narrows the list to the days with the words found and shows only those texts. "Hide the days" folds the list away.

**F12 > Guide** explains how the game works for a new player (the game came with a thick manual): endurance, strength and divine favor, money and reputation, towns and inns, healing, alchemy, saints, battle, travel and saving, with the game's own numbers and no story. The **tasks** in the margin are a short title each ("Raubritter @ Altenberg", "Debt Note from Lübeck") with the days left; a click opens one (the whole task, its deadline, and which way and about how many days away it is, and on the travel map Travel there) and closes it again, and a click on "by deadline" orders them by the nearest instead, or as taken. The journal's quest log has the same titles, opens the same way and keeps the same order. The world map (M) lists them too: a click shows the place, ready for Travel there. Each quest has a picture of its kind, in the margin, the journal and the world map's list, and on the map it marks the place: a great helm and crossed swords for a robber knight, a chest for a thing to fetch from a cave, tomb or lake, a sealed letter and a dagger for papers to get from someone in a town, a purse for a reward waiting (a bounty, or the thing brought back), the miners' hammer and pick for a mine's trouble, a dragon, the Wild Hunt's horn (the map's legend lists them all). On the travel map too, a quest's picture stands above its place. A town that bars the party (a lowered portcullis) or whose watch wants it (the pillory) is no longer listed among the tasks: its sign stands beside the town on the travel map and the world map, and the margin says so, with until when, while the party is there.

**One store room for every inn** (on by default, F12 > Settings > Helpers): what is left with one innkeeper is there at every other, like a chest that follows the party; stores already left in several towns join it the first time a store room is opened (it holds 100 kinds of things; a store that does not fit stays in its town until there is room), and F12 > Journal > Stored lists it once.

**Potions** have pictures of their own: one flask for all, in the potion's colour, with an emblem of what it does (a green cloud for Noxious Aroma, a bolt for Thunderbolt, a winged foot for Quickmove ...) and one, two or three gold dots for the grade of the formula that made it; the ingredients show the signs of the alchemists' tables (the planets' signs for the metals, the elements' triangles, a little man for the mandrake ...).

Fixed (bugs of 483.07, the "Card options greyed by mistake" switch): getting out over a city wall the guard could be offered a bribe the party could not pay; at a rebel crowd's fortress the climb with a rope was greyed when the party had a rope; one wall climb counted only the last member's rope; and at the wall by an old sally port each line from the second on did the next line's deed. On a card, an option shown greyed has a ? where its number would be, and the pointer on it says why it cannot be chosen now and what makes it possible (the guild will not see you again today, the gate must be broken first, you cannot afford the toll, the last member cannot retire ...): about 280 reasons read from the game's card scripts, a few of them oddities of the original kept as they are and named as such. On a card, a potion or saint in an option's list that cannot be chosen says why (the member knows the formula but carries none; at the easiest level the leader needs none), an option that starts a fight is marked with two crossed swords, and a text in a narrow column beside a picture starts with a plain letter instead of the large initial (which left a word or two a line). During the loot after a battle the margin still shows where the party is and its tasks (it said "None taken on"). The town margin no longer lists a gunsmith or jeweller: no card leads to either (the bowyer sells the guns; Jewelers' Avenue was cut from the game, its card unfinished). The Darklands+ window (F12) gathers Helpers, Mods, Display and About under **Settings**, so its tabs fit in a larger face, and a long page scrolls under its tabs instead of with them; the Darklands+ menu's Guide opens the guide (it opened the window at its last page). In a battle each member's order is a word on the party panel (attacks, parries, waits ...), and the left margin is framed like the others. Under a town's name the margin shows, as pictures, what it has that most towns lack (a university, a cathedral, an alchemist, a physician, docks with boats, a bank; what each offers under the pointer) and its **notable shops** (wares of 30q and better); every quality is written like 29q, and a shop's header now gives its wares' quality (it gave the shop's rating: a 33 swordsmith sells 29q). The clothmaker is no longer listed (its street only offers to leave). A member who carries too much has a weight on their card in the party panel instead of a list in the margin. Equipped things are marked red at their left in the shop, loot and store room lists. The margins' tooltips (a purse's total, why a town bars the party) and the party panel's (a member's order in a battle) now show; they never did. The hints for the menus and the guide stay at the foot of the left margin. The marching order moves a member with arrows (it said up and dn), and opened from the Party menu outside a battle it no longer turns the margins into a battle's. Foes who join a battle later get the next number of their kind (two could both be "Villager 1"). The world map's panel finds **towns with** a university, a cathedral, an alchemist, a physician, docks, a bank, or a swordsmith, armourer, bowyer or artificer of 30q and better: each is ringed on the map with its picture, and listed (a click shows it). With one pack for the party, the loot screen and the store room no longer have a tab for each member: the right list is the party's pack (each potion marked with whose it is), and a weapon or an armour under the pointer is set against the whole party (who wears something softer there, everyone's skill with that weapon, whom it is too heavy for). In a battle, the pointer on a member's order says what it does (and, for one who waits, how to give an order).

**Where to learn a saint:** a library (a town's monastery or university, or a monastery in the country) teaches four saints that the game draws from the party's seed and the place, so they are fixed for the whole game; F12 > Saints names, for each saint nobody in the party knows, the nearest library that teaches it (and all of them when it is opened), and the margin and the world map's tooltip list what a place's library teaches, the saints new to the party marked. The **start screen** is drawn anew, laid out like a card: the Darklands lettering of the original screen over the parchment at the left, the castle at dusk from the road to a town at the right; **Continue** loads the newest save at once (where, when, who and when it was written are shown), Load a saved game opens the list of saves (the whole screen), A new party and Quickstart are the game's own, Settings opens Darklands+'s window; Enter continues, L, N and Q as named.

**Making a character** fills the screen in larger type, and shows each thing once: the step's own rows on the left, the rest on the right. The profession step lists all 37 professions by kind (arms, nobility, church, trade, learning, crafts, common folk), the open ones dark; the one under the pointer shows what it needs (the game's own rules in words), what this character lacks for it ("Short of Intellect 20 (has 14)", "Not with this character's past"), what it leads to, its EPs, the attributes after it and, per skill, what it teaches and the room the skill EPs have afterwards (the 3:6 of before). A childhood says which professions it opens at first. The skill step shows each skill's room beside its value.

**Where to buy a formula:** each town's alchemist sells four formulae, likewise fixed for the whole game (only those of the first seven potions, Noxious Aroma to Sunburst, and one of Thunderbolt are ever sold); F12 > Alchemy names, for each formula nobody in the party knows, the nearest alchemist selling it and its price (all of them under the pointer), and the world map's tooltip on a town lists what its alchemist sells. Under the hood: docs/PORT_STRUCTURE.md.

**Presets** (F12 > Settings > Helpers, and the first start's welcome): **Buggy Purist** is the game exactly as it was (the old "Play the original game"); **Purist** adds only the fixes of the game's bugs; **Purist + interface** the screens and helpers that show more but change no rule; **Semi-Purist** the conveniences too; **Darklands+** (recommended) everything new as it is meant; **Easy** some gentler rules on top. Every switch is in one table, by section, each with a line on what it does; a choice of your own is kept and can be had back after trying a preset.

**Teachers:** a teacher is hired for a week and teaches at the inn ("Train or study"): each day a member pays the fee and, with a chance that grows with the teacher's ability, gains a point, up to the teacher's own ability (the "teachcap" fix: the game's texts say a teacher cannot take a skill beyond it, though the game never did so; a member who has reached it rests instead, and the inn greys that teacher with "learnt all"). The cards that bring one are marked with a lectern: a guild's masters ("request a meeting with the guild's masters", by day, to a party of some standing; Edged and Impact at the blacksmiths, Edged at the swordsmiths, Edged and Artifice at the armourers, one of Bows, Thrown or Missile devices at the bowyers', Artifice at the artificers'), the university (Alchemy, Religion, Latin, Read & Write, Healing), the monks (Religion, Latin, Read & Write, while no other teacher is hired in the town), the physician (Healing, unless the party heals better than he does, and not in some years) and the alchemists (Alchemy). Pointing at the lectern says what, how well, the fee the inn will charge (the card's quoted price is not it) and what it takes. The margin lists a town's teachers, those hired now and until when; the map's tooltip and Go To show them too, a lectern dimmed where they will not teach the party now. The inn's list says each teacher's ability and chance.

**Battles:** the party's moves and the enemies' are planned by a new pathfinder (enemies stuck at the back of a melee re-plan around everyone). When a member's foe falls, the game may send the member after whoever attacks the most wounded friend, however far: around the whole melee. Now a foe much farther than the nearest is passed over (Next foe within reach). A member who reloads or aims a bow, crossbow, gun or the next thrown weapon has a thin amber line under the bar, filling up. A skill raised in the fight rises in gold over the fighter's head (+2 Edged), and the note in the member's box flashes. Bars show only over the hurt (Alt shows them all). A click on a foe sends a member who is better with the missile weapon than with the weapon for blows to shoot (Use Missile), unless the foe is already within reach of a blow or the line is blocked. A click a little beside a foe, on the top of its head or at its feet (its spot is 8 pixels wide, the figure's arms and weapon reach beyond) is taken as on the foe, and a spot on the ground clicked before it in the same order no longer sends the member walking to beside the foe. When the last foe falls the field stands still a second, not three and a half, before the loot. The right button (or the middle one) held and moved drags the battle's view along, a step of the game's view at a time; a right click without moving still opens the menus. A step of the view (32 pixels across, 16 up or down) slides the field to its new place in an eighth of a second instead of jumping, the bars and figures' marks with it (Smooth scrolling). 1 to 5 choose a member without moving the view while the member is in sight (the game's view followed the member and jumped whenever they stood near an edge); pressed twice, the view goes to the member and follows.

**Potions** say what they do, from the game's code: Deadly Blade adds 3 to 5 to the damage and Strongedge 3 to 5 to the penetration of every weapon the member uses (bows and thrown ones too), for about a day with the potions fix; Greatpower raises the quality of the weapon in hand by 14, 22 or 30 (up to 99) with the greatpower fix (the game added those points to the damage and penetration bonuses: +14 penetration, or +1 damage and +6 or +14 penetration); St. Dunstan's +1 and St. Patrick's +2 armour, and Firewall's protection against fire last about a day with the armourday fix (the game never took them off, and the armour piled up); Trueflight raises Bows, Thrown and Missile devices. Each oil works with the weapons the manual names (the oilweapons fix): Deadly Blade with all but impact weapons and flails, Strongedge with blades and polearms, Great Power with impact weapons, flails and handguns (the game gave every oil to whatever weapon the member struck with). Hardarmor does what the manual says (armourday): armour 20, 25 or 30 points better in quality and 1, 2 or 2 levels thicker for a day (the game gave 1 to 3 levels and no quality, for good); a second keeps the stronger. New-wind, Ironarm, Truesight and Quickmove say plainly that they raise the value now, past the maximum if need be, and that on a wounded member New-wind and Ironarm heal. Fixed (a bug of the previews): with the potions fix on, oils drunk before a battle did nothing in it (the game puts its events away while a battle runs), and one drunk in a battle ended with it; both now last their day. The margin's "In force" lists Hardarmor, the saints' armour and Firewall too. Fixed (a bug of 483.07, the "standings" switch): the party's standing with the Fugger, the Medici and the Hanse (their cards raise and lower it; below nothing, with the town's regard, they give no tasks) was not in a saved game, so a load kept the last game's, or none after a restart; it is now kept in room the save's header leaves empty (the original game still reads such saves). Fixed (a bug of 483.07, the "battlesaints" switch): a battle puts the game's list of what is running away and reads it back afterwards over most of what the fight added to it, so a saint called in a battle raised attributes and skills for good as a rule (and the price some saints ask was never given back); such help now lasts its day. Fixed (a bug of 483.07, the "healboost" switch): healing (Essence o'Grace, a saint's) brings an attribute part of the way back to its maximum, from above too, so Essence o'Grace drunk while Ironarm ran took most of the boost away; healing now only raises. The pictures were redrawn: full flasks, every emblem inside the glass, and colours that stand apart. Fixed (bugs of 483.07, saints): St. Paul the Apostle gave what the texts and the manual give St. Paul the Simple (all lost Strength and Endurance healed, half the Intelligence lost for a day; virtue 70) and Paul the Simple a copy of St. Thomas Aquinas's help; the Apostle's own help (Speak Common, Latin, Read & Write, Charisma, Endurance; virtue 19) was in the game but never called ("pauls": each now does what its text says). St. John Nepomuk called on in Prag passed the reputation on to a place picked from whatever lay in memory, and St. Cecilia called on away from any town wrote it into "location -1", memory before the list of places ("saintrep": Prag gets Nepomuk's, the nearest town Cecilia's). Each saint now shows, beside its description, what its help does in the game's code ("What it does", in blue: the numbers, for how long, which towns' reputation), where the description promises otherwise ("Unlike the text": faster travel no code gives, reputation in lands not in the game, Flails where the text says Riding ...), and whether encounters call for it (its other powers work only there); the description itself is no longer coloured as if every line were an effect. All of it, for the 136 saints, is in docs/SAINTS.md. St. Crispin added 10 to the quality of every member's worn armour for good, piling up, and the armour in use then no longer matched its record in the pack; with armourday it counts 10 better in each hit for a day instead. St. Polycarp's fire protection (90 for everyone) stayed for good, and he, St. Charity, St. Erasmus and St. Pantaleon wrote their protection against fire into the bits the battle counts as armour (+32 or +48 for good, or nothing with the fireward fix); with armourday the member (Polycarp: everyone) has fire protection 90 for a day. F12 > Alchemy shows all 23 ingredients in the same places, those the party lacks greyed (it hid them behind a button).

**Lost for good:** whatever lowers an attribute's maximum or a skill for good (a card, a curse ...), armour worn down or ruined by acid or fire, and the years' toll are told in a window to click through, the game waiting. **Aging:** the game ages characters only while they are made (each five years past thirty cost Endurance, Strength, Agility, Perception, Intellect and Charisma by its table); now that table is spread month by month in play, told as it happens.

**Towns:** the world map washes out the towns the party has not visited, and their saints and formulae are known once it has (a switch shows them anyway); the quests' places glow. The margin gives the hour by the church bells, as the game's cards name it (Matins, Lauds, Prime, Terce, Sext, None, Vespers, Compline), and lists "Saints taught here" and "Formulae sold here" with their prices. Go To reaches the town hall (the council), the city prison and the barracks through the town square, chooses each option by its number (it used to land in the wrong street now and then), and remembers how skilled the town's physician is and to what quality its alchemists brew, once the party has learnt it. Beside each giver of tasks, Go To shows whether it has work: the chance that asking brings a task (the Fugger and the Medici: the town's regard, the company's standing and fame, 11 to 51 in 100; the Hanse alike; the canon: the regard less 50; the council or the lord: the regard, the speaker's Charisma and Speak Common; the market stalls: about one visit in seven), or until when none (a refusal, or a reward paid, shuts a door for three days; an audience tried, the council's or the lord's for a week), or that a task for them runs or its reward waits. Whether a task comes is a roll each time: only the chance can be known. Go To is redrawn: each place has a picture (21 new ones: the stalls, the pawnshop, the banks' arms, the inn, the square, the prison, the barracks, the gate ...) and sits in one of five groups (Merchants: the market stalls, the pawnshop and the guilds' streets; Quest givers: the banks, the Hanse, the town hall and the fortress; Religion; Learning and healing: the university, the physician, the alchemists; Around town), several to a row as the window allows. Every place keeps its tile in every town, greyed (its picture faded, not clickable) where the town lacks it, so each place is always in the same spot. Each place is a tile with a line for each thing that matters now: "31% chance of a task", "No tasks until 12 Mar", "Your reward is waiting", "On a task for them", the party's own task there ("Your task: ..."), the teacher ("Teaches Edged to 34", greyed when not now), a shop's wares ("Wares 29q", "better than most" from 30q) and what the party learnt of the physician or the alchemists; a row is as tall as its fullest tile, and a lectern and a task's picture beside the name mark a teacher and a task. The banks' pictures are their arms as about 1400: the Fugger's two lilies counterchanged on gold and blue, the Medici's seven red balls in an orle on gold (the blue ball with France's lilies only came in 1465); the Hanse, which bore no arms, a cog under a red and white sail. Under the pointer: what the place is (from its cards), the wares' quality, the task giver's terms in plain words, the party's tasks there, the teacher. Fixed (a bug of 483.07, the "cardopts" switch): Clothmakers' Street by day offered nothing but "leave": its script switches its shopfronts off (every other guild's street shows them, and the handler that opens the clothmaker's shop is there), so the clothmaker (clothing and soft armour: padded, leather, studded leather, cuir bouilli) sold only at night, to a sleeper woken there at the cost of a point of reputation. The shopfronts now open by day; Go To lists the street (without the fix it leaves it out, a walk there being for nothing), and the margin names a clothmaker of 30q or better among the notable shops.

Fixed (bugs of 483.07): a cathedral's canon gave out errands as if for the town's merchants: the merchants paid for them and offered none of their own meanwhile, the canon gave a new one at every visit, and the bishop's relic for a finished task could be taken again and again; now the errand is the bishop's, his relic paid once. A pagan altar the party had destroyed vanishes from the map but could still be chosen as an errand's place, which could then never be done; it is no longer chosen, and errands already sent to one go to the nearest altar still standing. In a tomb, taking the thing an errand seeks wakes its dead: winning that fight gave nothing and losing it gave the thing; now the winner has it. In a town without a council the lord of the fortress gives robber knights to hunt, but the game pays such a reward only at a council's hall, which that town does not have: the lord now pays it at an audience. Choosing a figure for a member (Select Character Image) shows under each figure the weapons it has animations of its own for (the enemies' figures have only those of their own weapons; another weapon is shown with the nearest).

**The opening:** a click during the gargoyles goes on to the lady's welcome; the opening's music, one piece timed to the animations, is stopped there (it went on under her words).
