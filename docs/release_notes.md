Darklands+ runs Darklands (MicroProse, 1992) natively on Windows, without DOSBox, from your own copy of the game. It adds the things players have asked for over the years, and most additions can be switched off. **F12 > Settings** offers presets, from **Full Original** (the game as it played in 1993, bugs and all; the look stays yours to choose) and **Purist** (the same with its bugs fixed) to the standard **Darklands+**, and every switch one by one; easier play is offered as built-in mods in **F12 > Mods**.

**You need** your own Darklands 483.07 (GOG or Steam) and 64-bit Windows 10 or 11. This download contains nothing from the game.

**Install:** unpack the zip anywhere and start `darklands.exe`. It finds a GOG or Steam installation by itself or asks for the folder once. The first start takes about two minutes while Darklands+ translates the game program from your `DARKLAND.EXE`; after that it starts at once. Your saved games stay where they always were. `Darklands+ README.txt` in the zip has the details.

**New in 1.3**

**Battles**

- **Improved combat** (on by default): the party fights on sensibly between orders. Shooters open fire by themselves, take a new mark when theirs falls, step aside for a clear line, and draw their melee weapon when a foe closes in or a friend a few paces off is fighting one. Members with no orders join a fight beside them. One switch replaces 1.2's separate shooting switches.
- **Orders in one click:** select a member (on the field or their box), then click an enemy or a spot. The orders are buttons with pictures at the foot of the field, a bow or sword on each shooter's box chooses shooting or melee, a label by the pointer says what a click will do, and a shot's line shows what blocks it.
- **Walking in a crowd:** fighters no longer stand still for seconds behind someone, enemies no longer take long detours, and planning their routes no longer makes the battle stutter.
- **Battle info:** the to-hit numbers are the ones the game uses (1.2 had attack and defense swapped), the weapons named are the ones in hand (1.2 named others), and each of your weapons against an enemy's armor is colored: pierces, half damage, or mostly deflected.

**Cards, towns and the party**

- **Cards say who tries an option and what it rests on:** the member's head, the skills and attributes as heraldic shields, the town's opinion and the party's fame. Where any member may speak for the party, a click on another member's chance has them speak (an alchemist's formula trade too). An option on the way to one of the party's quests in town (a reward waiting, papers to take from an office) carries the quest's picture, and the town gate warns when its guards may stop the party.
- **Screens redrawn in period style:** alchemy as an alchemist's workbench with the Philosopher's Stone pictured; the inn and camp as a ledger that names the potions made and shows the hours going by (1.2's page looked frozen); the shop at its counter, the store room in a cellar, Party Info, Save and Load, marching order and enemy info.
- **Shops and the character sheet:** items in sections; the load a weapon or armor would bring before you buy it; ingredients ten at a time, with how many the party has; a spare horse given to a member with Ride, a drag, or their number. Items can be marked Keep or Sell, and the shop's Sell spare gear sells those marked Sell.
- **Ammunition that runs out** (with the shared pack): each member's arrows, quarrels, balls and thrown weapons are their own.
- **"Lost for good"** no longer shows after a battle for a member whose skill a saint has raised for a while (1.2 showed it after every battle, the boosted skills "lowered").
- **Go to** works by night, says why a place is shut, and shows the party walking instead of flashing the cards on the way.
- **Calling on a saint:** the favor to spend on a bar with the best chance marked, the reason when there is no chance, the Virtue each saint asks, and prayers from the character sheet.
- **Docks:** a small map of where the boats go, with each fare. **More day jobs:** nine jobs the original never offers turn up in towns with the right places.
- **Right margin:** the party's fame, the town's reputation with what changed it, whether its watch is on alert, and the formulae and teachers to be had in town as pictures.

**The world map**

- **Quests and towns live on the world map** (the Journal's pages for them are gone): boat routes along the rivers and coasts, windows to find alchemical ingredients or any ware, towns on wax seals, several quests at one place circling it, the way the party would take, Snap to party, and a view that stays on the map.

**Look and sound**

- **Period ornaments:** plaited frames and gilt corners, clocks to choose for passing time, skills and attributes as heraldic shields everywhere, ingredients as the alchemists' signs, potion grades as chevrons, and Comic Sans as an eighth typeface (we are so sorry).
- **Tooltips** open beside what they describe, never over it or the pointer.
- **Sound:** the whole soundtrack to play in Settings; previews of each device's music and battle sounds; evener music timing; the PC speaker heard at last (1.2 played nothing on it). The MT-32 and the CM-32L are one choice, and MIDI out is a device of its own.

**Settings and saving**

- **One Settings page** with a picture beside each section. Presets are Full Original, Purist and Darklands+; easier play comes as built-in mods (F12 > Mods, with a load order to drag).
- **Saving and loading** in the game's own Save and Load screens, redrawn; F12 no longer has a Saves page. The first start of each version backs your saves up.
- **Ironman** (for a new game): one save that the game keeps itself, with no loading back.
- **Controller button prompts**, drawn as an Xbox, PlayStation or Nintendo controller has them.

**Restored content** (F12 > Reconstructed Content, experimental, off by default)

- City politics, cities at war, the "Fehde!" ambush and a town's hospice: written by MicroProse and never switched on.

**Bugs of the original game**

- 23 more fixed (60 in all), most from the list Arkana Mechanika (Darklands Restoration) keep: climbing a city wall at night, mounts that never helped outrun wolves, a church's tenth that blessed nobody, an audience "fixed for tomorrow" that never came, bands of enemies missing from battles, shots stopped by clouds not in the way, loot lost past 255 of a kind, ordinary hits that made no sound, and more.
- The cave south-east of Basel, which lies off the map, is moved to where the party can walk onto it (1.2 had moved it onto the map's last row, which the party cannot walk onto).

**Withdrawn**

- The New Player Guide, until every statement in it has been checked in play.

### What it adds

- **Text and display:** crisp text at any window size up to 4K, in a choice of eight typefaces (a readable gothic one, Grenze Gotisch, by default; the eighth is Comic Sans, we are so sorry). The pointer follows the mouse smoothly. Wide-screen margins show the date, place, purse and quests on one side, and on the other the town you are in or near (reputation, what it has that most towns lack, its notable shops), and the blessings and potions at work; a member who carries too much has a weight on their card. One setting sizes the margins, tooltips and map names. The window opens maximized (it remembers if you make it smaller). Full screen with Alt+Enter. PNG screenshots with Ctrl+F12 (it also writes the window sizes into the log, for reports). The whole opening (the MicroProse logo and the animations) plays at each start: a key or a click goes on at once to its last scene, the lady of the Darklands and her welcome, and another to the title (F12 > Settings > Intro: the whole opening, the last scene or nothing). The title menu shows the Darklands+ version.
- **No more changing the leader:** a card's test of the leader alone (every talk with its Charisma and Speak Common, a lore, a perception ...) is made by the member who does it best, whose name and chance are shown beside the option, and shops price by the party's best Charisma ("Ursula bargains"); the leader you chose is back after the card. F12 > Settings.
- **One pack for the whole party:** each member keeps only what they use (and the horse or mule they ride) and their own potions; everything else is the party's pack, every spare item of every member by kind and quality. The sheet shows In use, then Potions, then the pack. Ready takes from the pack (Darklands+ hands the item over first), shops list and sell the pack (Sell spare gear sells the whole party's), the loot and an inn's store room show the member's potions and the pack beside the pile or the store (a potion goes to the member shown, the rest into the pack; Put away stores the pack but what is kept and the potions). The game itself still keeps each item with some member, so where it asks one member for something Darklands+ hands it over first: lock picks to whoever opens a lock, a spare horse or mule to whoever has none (so the party counts as mounted). A member who leaves, or dies on a card, leaves their spares in the pack. With ammunition that never runs out, arrows, quarrels and balls are not shown at all (each shooter quietly carries the one the game needs). **Keep** marks an item never to be sold or dropped. Pointing at the load under the doll explains what it does (only what is in use weighs: Agility in card tests and in fending off blows, the pace of swings). F12 > Settings.
- **Money with coins:** every amount Darklands+ writes (the purse, shops, inns, the loot, the journal, Party Info) shows its florins, groschen and pfennigs each with a small gold, silver or copper coin, and a price described below a shop's lists (or the purse under the pointer) also gives the whole amount in pfennigs, so two prices compare at a glance (1 florin = 20 groschen = 240 pfennigs). F12 > Settings > Text switches back to 1fl 12gr 4pf.
- **Tooltips everywhere:** point at an item, a saint or an alchemy formula on any of the game's screens (shops, loot, character sheet, equipment, saints lists, the formula book, a card's list of saints and items) to see what it does. Saint descriptions show the effect in its own color.
- **Cards:** drawn by Darklands+ (the text in the chosen typeface, the options as rows to click, an illuminated initial; switchable). Chances of success next to the options (only where the party's skills decide). Number keys pick options. The saints and items a card accepts are shown. "Go to" in cities (G) walks the party to a shop, church or gate.
- **Journal:** a chronicle of every text the cards have shown (word for word, with the date, the hour, the place and your choice, searchable, with a list of the days by month and their places to leaf through), your own notes for each game, what the party has left in store rooms anywhere, the battle log and the Honor Roll. The quests are listed on the world map (M), grouped by kind (a witches' Sabbat too, once a card has told the party its night or its place, the place marked on the map), with a record of each town and, behind "Find alchemical ingredients" and "Find any ware", where to find ingredients and goods. Saints and formulae are on the character sheet.
- **Travel:** a zoomable world map (M) and auto-travel: Ctrl+click a place and the party goes there. Towns and places are named in the overland view. Random encounters on the road can be made rarer (or more frequent). So can the cutthroats and the watch in a town's streets at night (both built-in mods, F12 > Mods): with little Stealth and Streetwise, a party leaving the side streets at night meets thieves about 8 times in 10.
- **Battles:** a battle log, info under the pointer (an enemy's weapons, its armor and whether each of your weapons pierces it, is halved or deflected), a click on an enemy sends the selected member to attack it (Std Attack) without first choosing an attack from the order menu, a thin bar over each fighter with the Endurance left (and the Strength once it is down by half), a pause when someone falls, adjustable battle speed, better pathfinding, and a map of the level (Tab), where a click on a floor, a door or stairs the party has seen walks the party there (in single file, the leader first, so that a passage or a wall does not jam the formation; G travels as a group again). A shooter whose line of fire is blocked (by a friend, an enemy or a corner) steps aside to the nearest spot a few paces away with a clear line and shoots from there, instead of waiting; any enemy can be chosen as the target even with no clear line (the game refused it with a barred circle and a beep): with no spot near, the shooter walks towards it until a line opens (part of Improved combat, on by default). If you like, the party's archers, crossbowmen and gunners shoot past their own fighters instead (in Darklands a missile only ever strikes its target; off by default). **Z** fast-forwards a battle (six times the pace, for the easy fights) and Z again returns to the chosen speed. In a castle, dungeon or mine the margin also says how many enemies still wait there, not yet met, on this floor and on the others, and marks the master of the place, or says on which floor he waits (once he has fallen and his fight is over, the place is won: the game ends a castle's or dungeon's battle only so, or when the party walks out and flees). At the end, the box the game shows ("BattleResults - victory", the field held still for eight seconds) says "Victory! The battle is won." and stays three seconds.
- **The character sheet does it all:** items, skills, saints and formulae of the whole party in the game's own screen: drag and drop between members, a gold dot for the party's best at each skill (everyone's value under the pointer), what a saint raised in blue with how long it lasts, every formula with its ingredients, batches, chances and where it is sold, all 23 ingredients, a weapon's or armor's full numbers and who can use it, a one-click leader change, each pack in order by kind and name. On the loot screen, A takes the whole pile and S shares it out among the party, instead of one G per arrow; in shops, A sells a whole stack and T buys ten, and **Sell spare gear** sells, after a second click, every weapon, armor and shield the member carries but does not use (not ammunition or thrown weapons), with the total shown first.
- **More sprites for the party:** Select Character Image offers the enemies' human sprites too: the grey-haired, brown-haired and blond ex-adventurers, a sergeant, town guard, mercenary, bandit, knight, knight in plate, Templar, monk, alchemist, witch and cultist (18 sprites instead of 4), all shown at once to pick from. Each walks, fights and dies with its own animations and has colors of its own for the 1st/2nd/3rd color keys (red, blue, green, gold ...; hair keeps its color). A sprite without an animation for the weapon in hand swings the nearest one it has.
- **Saves:** quick save (Ctrl+S) and quick load (F9), autosaves on arrival and before battles, and a save browser.

  - weapon potions that never wear off
  - quests off the edge of the map
  - robbers corrupting the item count (a crash later)

- **Saints that do what their texts say:** 39 saints whose coded help strayed from their description follow it now: faster travel for a week (St. Christopher, St. Drogo, St. Wilfrid; St. Eustace through woods), the party's protection against fire, better weapons and armor, Riding where the game gave Flails, reputation in the saint's own lands. Every saint shows what it really does beside its text (docs/SAINTS.md: the whole table and the reasons).
- **Restored:** the Honor Roll of Glory, drawn by MicroProse but never reachable (F12 > Reconstructed Content).

Give it the synthesizer's two ROM files (if you use Munt or DOSBox with an MT-32 you have them; Darklands+ finds them in Munt's and DOSBox Staging's ROM folders by itself) and the music and battle sounds play as composed, with no MIDI setup. Without the ROMs the emulated Sound Blaster plays, and Darklands+ explains at start why the MT-32 is worth it and lets you pick the ROMs' folder (with "Don't remind me again"). With both a CM-32L and an MT-32 pair, the MT-32 plays. What the game writes on the synthesizer's display ("[ MPS Darklands ]") is shown for a moment, as on the real one.

While it is in use the screens show its buttons. Its settings are in F12 > Settings > Controller.

- **Music volume:** its own slider (F12 > Settings > Sound), for the FM and the MT-32 alike.

A small red seal on the card marks a new one. Mods can add their own: an encounter is a text file with a short script (docs/MODDING.md).
- **Modding:** mod folders for game data, single card texts, menu texts, weapon numbers, items, pictures and sounds; mods switch on and off at once, sounds included. F12 > Mods makes a new mod folder with instructions.

### Limits kept from the original

These are part of the game's memory and of every saved game, so Darklands+ works within them rather than changing them (a change would break existing saves):

- 64 kinds of item per member, each kind a stack of up to 255 (with one pack for the party, that is 64 kinds for each member, and new items go to whoever has room);
- 100 kinds of item in a town's store room (more is refused, with a message saying why);
- 20 kinds of throwing potion in a member's battle orders;
- five members in the party.

### If something goes wrong

Darklands+ writes `darklands_port.log` in the game folder; it says why a start failed. Report problems on GitHub, under Issues, with that log (or `darklands_port_previous.log`, the session before, if you have started the game again since) and, if you can, a save from just before the problem. Some antivirus programs distrust files a compiler has just made. If yours blocks `darklands_game.dll`, allow it; it is made from your own `DARKLAND.EXE` by the TinyCC in `darklands_plus\tcc`. On Linux and Steam Deck the Windows zip runs under Wine or Proton.
