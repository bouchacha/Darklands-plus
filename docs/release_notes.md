Darklands+ runs Darklands (MicroProse, 1992) natively on Windows, without DOSBox, from your own copy of the game. It adds the things players have asked for over the years, and most additions can be switched off. **F12 > Settings** offers presets, from **Full Original** (the game as it played in 1993, bugs and all; the look stays yours to choose) and **Purist** (the same with its bugs fixed) to the standard **Darklands+**, and every switch one by one; easier play is offered as built-in mods in **F12 > Mods**.

**You need** your own Darklands 483.07 (GOG or Steam) and 64-bit Windows 10 or 11. This download contains nothing from the game.

**Install:** unpack the zip anywhere and start `darklands.exe`. It finds a GOG or Steam installation by itself or asks for the folder once. The first start takes about two minutes while Darklands+ translates the game program from your `DARKLAND.EXE`; after that it starts at once. Your saved games stay where they always were. `Darklands+ README.txt` in the zip has the details.

### What it adds

- **Text and display:** crisp text at any window size up to 4K, in a choice of eight typefaces (a readable gothic one, Grenze Gotisch, by default; the eighth is Comic Sans, we are so sorry). The pointer follows the mouse smoothly. Wide-screen margins show the date, place, purse and quests on one side, and on the other the town you are in or near (reputation, what it has that most towns lack, its notable shops), and the blessings and potions at work; a member who carries too much has a weight on their card. One setting sizes the margins, tooltips and map names. The window opens maximized (it remembers if you make it smaller). Full screen with Alt+Enter. PNG screenshots with Ctrl+F12 (it also writes the window sizes into the log, for reports). The whole opening (the MicroProse logo and the animations) plays at each start: a key or a click goes on at once to its last scene, the lady of the Darklands and her welcome, and another to the title (F12 > Settings > Intro: the whole opening, the last scene or nothing). The title menu shows the Darklands+ version.
- **No more changing the leader:** a card's test of the leader alone (every talk with its Charisma and Speak Common, a lore, a perception ...) is made by the member who does it best, whose name and chance are shown beside the option, and shops price by the party's best Charisma ("Ursula bargains"); the leader you chose is back after the card. F12 > Settings.
- **One pack for the whole party:** each member keeps only what they use (and the horse or mule they ride) and their own potions; everything else is the party's pack, every spare item of every member by kind and quality. The sheet shows In use, then Potions, then the pack. Ready takes from the pack (Darklands+ hands the item over first), shops list and sell the pack (Sell spare gear sells the whole party's), the loot and an inn's store room show the member's potions and the pack beside the pile or the store (a potion goes to the member shown, the rest into the pack; Put away stores the pack but what is kept and the potions). The game itself still keeps each item with some member, so where it asks one member for something Darklands+ hands it over first: lock picks to whoever opens a lock, a spare horse or mule to whoever has none (so the party counts as mounted). A member who leaves, or dies on a card, leaves their spares in the pack. With ammunition that never runs out, arrows, quarrels and balls are not shown at all (each shooter quietly carries the one the game needs). **Keep** marks an item never to be sold or dropped. Pointing at the load under the doll explains what it does (only what is in use weighs: Agility in card tests and in fending off blows, the pace of swings). F12 > Settings.
- **Money with coins:** every amount Darklands+ writes (the purse, shops, inns, the loot, the journal, Party Info) shows its florins, groschen and pfennigs each with a small gold, silver or copper coin, and a price described below a shop's lists (or the purse under the pointer) also gives the whole amount in pfennigs, so two prices compare at a glance (1 florin = 20 groschen = 240 pfennigs). F12 > Settings > Text switches back to 1fl 12gr 4pf.
- **Tooltips everywhere:** point at an item, a saint or an alchemy formula on any of the game's screens (shops, loot, character sheet, equipment, saints lists, the formula book, a card's list of saints and items) to see what it does. Saint descriptions show the effect in its own color.
- **Cards:** drawn by Darklands+ (the text in the chosen typeface, the options as rows to click, an illuminated initial; switchable). Chances of success next to the options (only where the party's skills decide). Number keys pick options. The saints and items a card accepts are shown. "Go to" in cities (G) walks the party to a shop, church or gate.
- **Journal:** a chronicle of every text the cards have shown (word for word, with the date, the hour, the place and your choice, searchable, with a list of the days by month and their places to leaf through), your own notes for each game, what the party has left in store rooms anywhere, the battle log and the Honor Roll. The quests are listed on the world map (M), grouped by kind (a witches' Sabbat too, once a card has told the party its night or its place, the place marked on the map), with a record of each town and, under Find... ("Alchemical ingredients", "Any ware"), where to find ingredients and goods. Saints and formulae are on the character sheet.
- **Travel:** a zoomable world map (M) and auto-travel: Ctrl+click a place and the party goes there. The campfire under the clock can stop the party to camp at 20:00. Towns and places are named in the overland view. Random encounters on the road can be made rarer (or more frequent). So can the cutthroats and the watch in a town's streets at night (both built-in mods, F12 > Mods): with little Stealth and Streetwise, a party leaving the side streets at night meets thieves about 8 times in 10.
- **Battles:** a battle log, info under the pointer (an enemy's weapons, its armor and whether each of your weapons pierces it, is halved or deflected), a click on an enemy sends the selected member to attack it (Std Attack) without first choosing an attack from the order menu, a thin bar over each fighter with the Endurance left (and the Strength once it is down by half), a pause when someone falls, adjustable battle speed, better pathfinding, and a map of the level (Tab), where a click on a floor, a door or stairs the party has seen walks the party there (in single file, the leader first, so that a passage or a wall does not jam the formation; G travels as a group again). A shooter whose line of fire is blocked (by a friend, an enemy or a corner) steps aside to the nearest spot a few paces away with a clear line and shoots from there, instead of waiting; any enemy can be chosen as the target even with no clear line (the game refused it with a barred circle and a beep): with no spot near, the shooter walks towards it until a line opens (part of Improved combat, on by default). If you like, the party's archers, crossbowmen and gunners shoot past their own fighters instead (in Darklands a missile only ever strikes its target; off by default). **Z** fast-forwards a battle (six times the pace, for the easy fights) and Z again returns to the chosen speed. In a castle, dungeon or mine the margin also says how many enemies still wait there, not yet met, on this floor and on the others, and marks the master of the place, or says on which floor he waits (once he has fallen and his fight is over, the place is won: the game ends a castle's or dungeon's battle only so, or when the party walks out and flees). At the end, the box the game shows ("BattleResults - victory", the field held still for eight seconds) says "Victory! The battle is won." and stays three seconds.

On the loot screen, A takes the whole pile and S shares it out among the party, instead of one G per arrow; in shops, A sells a whole stack and T buys ten, and **Sell spare gear** sells, after a second click, every weapon, armor and shield the member carries but does not use (not ammunition or thrown weapons), with the total shown first.
- **More sprites for the party:** Select Character Image offers the enemies' human sprites too: the grey-haired, brown-haired and blond ex-adventurers, a sergeant, town guard, mercenary, bandit, knight, knight in plate, Templar, monk, alchemist, witch and cultist (18 sprites instead of 4), all shown at once to pick from. Each walks, fights and dies with its own animations and has colors of its own for the 1st/2nd/3rd color keys (red, blue, green, gold ...; hair keeps its color). A sprite without an animation for the weapon in hand swings the nearest one it has.
- **Saves:** quick save (Ctrl+S) and quick load (F9), autosaves on arrival and before battles, and a save browser.
- **Fixes for bugs of 483.07:**
  - blessings that cost attribute points for good
  - items lost to a full pack
  - "(null)" items
  - the "neon hair" colors
  - Save and Load greyed out after the inn
  - weapon potions that never wear off
  - quests off the edge of the map
  - robbers corrupting the item count (a crash later)
  - items lost in overfull store rooms
  - and more, each with its own switch (F12 > Settings > Fixes)

Every saint shows what it really does beside its text (docs/SAINTS.md: the whole table and the reasons).
- **Restored:** the Honor Roll of Glory, drawn by MicroProse but never reachable (F12 > Reconstructed Content).

Give it the synthesizer's two ROM files (if you use Munt or DOSBox with an MT-32 you have them; Darklands+ finds them in Munt's and DOSBox Staging's ROM folders by itself) and the music and battle sounds play as composed, with no MIDI setup. With both a CM-32L and an MT-32 pair, the MT-32 plays unless you choose the CM-32L in F12 > Settings > Sound. ### Limits kept from the original

These are part of the game's memory and of every saved game, so Darklands+ works within them rather than changing them (a change would break existing saves):

- 64 kinds of item per member, each kind a stack of up to 255 (with one pack for the party, that is 64 kinds for each member, and new items go to whoever has room);
- 100 kinds of item in a town's store room (more is refused, with a message saying why);
- 20 kinds of throwing potion in a member's battle orders;
- five members in the party.

### If something goes wrong

Darklands+ writes `darklands_port.log` in the game folder; it says why a start failed. Report problems on GitHub, under Issues: F12 > About > Report a problem makes a zip with the logs and your two newest saves and opens the form (Darklands+ also makes one by itself when it sees something that must never happen); without it, attach that log (or `darklands_port_previous.log`, the session before, if you have started the game again since) and, if you can, a save from just before the problem. For a fault that is hard to repeat, tick "Detailed log, for testing" under F12 > About first. Some antivirus programs distrust files a compiler has just made. If yours blocks `darklands_game.dll`, allow it; it is made from your own `DARKLAND.EXE` by the TinyCC in `darklands_plus\tcc`. On Linux and Steam Deck the Windows zip runs under Wine or Proton.
