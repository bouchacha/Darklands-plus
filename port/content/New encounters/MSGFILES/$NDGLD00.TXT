Where the street narrows under the overhanging houses, two processions have met head-on: the butchers behind their banner of the ox, the tanners behind their banner of the scraping-knife, each with a guild candle as thick as a man's thigh, each bound for the minster for the feast. Neither will give way. Custom says one guild walks before the other, but nobody can agree which, and the shouting is turning ugly.

$ChosenOneName finds a toothless goodwife on a stool in a doorway, who has watched sixty years of processions go by. "Neither of them," she cackles. "In my mother's day the shoemakers walked first, ahead of the both of them."
\14There is a silence. Then a roar of outrage, aimed for once at the same men: the shoemakers, who are not even here. Butchers and tanners march off together, arm in arm, to have it out with the cobblers, and the beadle shakes your hands until they ache.
