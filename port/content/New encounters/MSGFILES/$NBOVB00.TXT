A cry goes up at the stern. The steersman's boy, a lad of ten, has slipped from the wet gunwale into the river. The current is fast and brown here, and it takes him at once: his head comes up, goes under, comes up again farther off. His father cannot leave the steering oar, and the other boatmen are poling hard at the bow.

As you pray, the current swirls and turns, and carries the boy straight back against the hull, where a boatman hooks him by the collar. He coughs up half the river and lives.

No sign is given. The boy's head goes under, and comes up again, farther away.
